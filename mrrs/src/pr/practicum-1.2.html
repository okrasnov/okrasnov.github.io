Assignment 1.2. Statistics of Real Radar Signals#

Section 1: Introduction#

In this practical assignment, you will investigate the statistical properties of real radar echo signals acquired by the PARSAX radar during observations of highway A13. The measured data contain a mixture of thermal noise, stationary clutter, moving vehicles, and strong reflectors with different radar cross sections (RCS).

Radar echoes from different physical environments often exhibit distinct statistical behaviors. Understanding these behaviors is essential for radar detection, clutter modeling, threshold selection, and target characterization.

The objectives of this assignment are:

  • Analyze the statistical properties of complex-valued radar measurements.

  • Estimate first- and second-order statistical moments from measured data.

  • Compare empirical distributions with theoretical statistical models.

  • Investigate how signal statistics change for different target and clutter scenarios.

  • Relate the observed distributions to physical scattering mechanisms discussed during the course.

  • Evaluate the validity and limitations of commonly used radar signal models.

Final Report Questions#

The following questions are suitable as mandatory report questions.

Q1. How many complex radar samples are available in the dataset, and why is a large sample size important when estimating probability density functions?

Q2. What differences can be observed between the linear-scale and dB-scale range-slow-time visualizations? Which representation provides better insight into weak radar echoes?

Q3. Which regions of the range-slow-time plot appear approximately stationary and which regions exhibit non-stationary behavior? Explain the physical reasons.

Q4. For the selected data subset, do the real and imaginary signal components follow a Gaussian distribution? Support your conclusion using histograms and estimated parameters.

Q5. Does the signal amplitude follow a Rayleigh distribution? Under what assumptions would a Rayleigh model be expected?

Q6. Is the phase distribution approximately uniform over [−π,π][-\pi,\pi]? What does this indicate about the underlying scattering process?

Q7. Which power model (Exponential, Swerling III, or neither) provides the best fit to the measured data? Justify your conclusion quantitatively and physically.

Q8. How do the statistical properties differ between:

  • noise-dominated regions,

  • stationary clutter,

  • strong reflectors,

  • moving vehicle scenarios?

Q9. Is the joint distribution of the in-phase and quadrature components circularly symmetric? Explain how this can be verified from the 2D histogram and covariance estimates.

Q10. To what extent can the measured radar echoes be modeled as circular complex Gaussian processes? Discuss both supporting evidence and observed deviations.

Q11. What physical scattering mechanisms can explain departures from the ideal Gaussian-Rayleigh-Exponential model?

Q12. Which statistical parameter or visualization was most useful for distinguishing between noise, clutter, and targets? Explain your reasoning.
